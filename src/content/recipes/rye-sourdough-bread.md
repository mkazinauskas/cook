## Ingredients

- 400 g dark (wholemeal) rye flour
- 360 g water, warm (about 35°C)
- 100 g active sourdough starter (fed 4-12 hours earlier and bubbly)
- 8 g salt
- Butter or oil, for the tin

## Instructions

1. Grease a 900 g (about 23x13 cm) loaf tin well with butter or oil.
2. Stir the starter into the water until it dissolves.
3. Add the flour and salt and mix with a spoon or spatula for a minute or two until there are no dry bits. It will be a thick, sticky paste, not a dough. That is normal: rye doesn't need kneading.
4. Scrape it into the tin and smooth the top with a wet spatula or wet hands. Dust the top with a little rye flour.
5. Cover and leave somewhere warm (around 26°C) for 3-5 hours, until it has risen by about a third and the floury top shows cracks and small holes.
6. Heat the oven to 240°C about 30 minutes before the dough is ready.
7. Bake 15 minutes at 240°C, then lower to 200°C and bake another 45 minutes, until dark brown and pulling away from the sides of the tin.
8. Turn the loaf out of the tin and cool on a wire rack. Wrap it in a tea towel once it is cold and wait until the next day to slice it. Rye stays sticky inside if cut early.

## Notes

A rye starter is best, but a regular wheat starter works too.

For a classic flavour, stir 1 tsp caraway or coriander seeds into the dough in step 3.

Keeps well wrapped for 5-7 days and gets better on day 2 and 3. Slice thinly.
