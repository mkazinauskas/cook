## Ingredients

- 500 g strong bread flour (12-13 g protein)
- 375 g water, lukewarm (about 27°C)
- 100 g active sourdough starter (100% hydration, fed 4-12 hours earlier and at its peak)
- 10 g salt
- Rice flour, for dusting the banneton

## Timeline

The bread takes two days, but only about 45 minutes of it is hands-on. A typical schedule:

- **Day 1, 8:00** – feed the starter
- **14:00** – mix flour and water (steps 1-2)
- **15:00-17:30** – add starter and salt, then stretch and fold (steps 3-5)
- **~20:00** – shape and put in the fridge overnight (steps 6-9)
- **Day 2, 9:00** – preheat the pot (step 10)
- **10:00** – bake, then cool until about 12:00 (steps 11-14)

## Instructions

1. Check the starter is ready: it should have at least doubled since feeding, be domed and bubbly, and a spoonful should float in water.
2. Mix the flour with all but a small splash of the water until no dry flour remains. Cover and rest 45-60 minutes (autolyse).
3. Add the starter and squeeze it through the dough with wet hands until fully combined. Rest 30 minutes.
4. Sprinkle over the salt with the reserved splash of water and pinch and fold until absorbed and the dough comes back together.
5. Let the dough rise in a warm spot (24-26°C) for 4-6 hours. Bakers call this the bulk ferment. During the first 2 hours do four sets of stretch and folds, 30 minutes apart: lift one side of the dough, fold it over the middle, turn the bowl a quarter and repeat on all four sides. Then leave it alone.
6. The rise is done when the dough has grown by 50-75%, has a domed, smooth top with bubbles on the sides and jiggles when you shake the bowl. Go by the dough, not the clock: a cooler kitchen takes longer.
7. Tip the dough onto an unfloured worktop and pre-shape into a loose round with a bench scraper. Rest uncovered 20-30 minutes.
8. Lightly flour the top, flip it over and shape it to match your pot: a tight round (boule) for a round pot, a short log (batard) for an oval one. Build surface tension by dragging it towards you on the worktop.
9. Dust a banneton (proofing basket), or a bowl lined with a tea towel, generously with rice flour. Place the dough in seam side up, cover and refrigerate 12-16 hours (up to 48).
10. Put the Dutch oven with its lid on a rack in the lower third of the oven and heat to 250°C for 45-60 minutes, so the cast iron is fully heated through.
11. Turn the cold dough straight out of the fridge onto a piece of baking paper and slash (score) the top in one confident stroke about 1 cm deep with a razor blade or very sharp knife.
12. Carefully take the pot out, lower the dough in using the paper as a sling, put the lid back on and bake 20 minutes at 250°C.
13. Remove the lid, lower the oven to 230°C and bake another 20-25 minutes, until the crust is deep brown and the loaf sounds hollow when tapped underneath (96-98°C inside).
14. Cool on a wire rack for at least 1 hour, better 2, before slicing. The crumb is still setting and will turn gummy if cut hot.

## Notes

The amounts make one loaf of about 1 kg, which suits a large Dutch oven of 24-26 cm (around 5 l). For a smaller 20-22 cm pot, use three quarters of every ingredient (375 g flour).

Check your pot's maximum oven temperature before preheating it empty. Some enamelled pots have lid knobs rated well below 250°C; swap in a metal knob or bake at 230°C throughout with 5 extra minutes uncovered.

New to sourdough or using weaker flour? Start at 70% hydration and work up once you are comfortable shaping wetter dough.

For a bit more flavour, swap 50 g of the bread flour for whole wheat or rye flour and add 10 g extra water.
