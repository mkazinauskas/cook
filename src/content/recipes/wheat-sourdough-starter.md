## Ingredients

- About 200 g whole wheat (wholemeal) flour
- About 1 kg strong bread flour
- Water, lukewarm (about 26°C). Use filtered or boiled-and-cooled tap water if yours is heavily chlorinated
- A clean 0.5-1 l glass jar with a loose lid, and a rubber band to mark the level

## Instructions

1. **Day 1:** In the jar, stir together 50 g whole wheat flour and 50 g water until no dry flour remains. Scrape down the sides, put the lid on loosely and leave somewhere warm (24-26°C) for 24 hours.
2. **Day 2:** You may see a few bubbles, or nothing at all. Both are fine. Stir and leave another 24 hours.
3. **Day 3:** Discard all but 50 g of the starter. Add 25 g whole wheat flour, 25 g bread flour and 50 g water, stir well, scrape down the sides and mark the level with the rubber band.
4. **Days 4-7:** Repeat the step 3 feed once a day, at roughly the same time. Around day 3-4 the starter often bubbles up dramatically, smells odd (cheesy, like nail polish) and then goes quiet. That first burst is bacteria, not yeast. Don't give up: keep feeding.
5. **From about day 7:** Once the starter rises noticeably within 12 hours of a feed, switch to two feeds a day, 12 hours apart, each time keeping 50 g and adding 50 g bread flour and 50 g water. The whole wheat flour is no longer needed.
6. **Ready:** The starter is ready to bake with when it reliably doubles within 4-8 hours of a feed for 2-3 days in a row, smells pleasantly sour and yeasty, and a spoonful floats in water. This usually takes 7-14 days.
7. To bake, feed it 4-12 hours before you need it and use it at its peak, when it is domed and bubbly and has at least doubled.

## Keeping the starter

- **Baking often:** keep it on the counter and feed twice a day (50 g starter + 50 g flour + 50 g water).
- **Baking weekly:** after a feed, let it rise for 1-2 hours, then keep it in the fridge. Feed once a week. The day before baking, take it out and give it 1-2 feeds at room temperature until it doubles again.
- Grey liquid (hooch) on top just means it is hungry. Pour it off or stir it in and feed.

## Notes

Weigh everything: a 100% hydration starter is equal weights of flour and water, which is what the bread recipes here expect.

The discard is not wasted: stir it into pancake, waffle or cracker batter, or keep it in a jar in the fridge for later.

Throw the starter out and start again if you see pink or orange streaks or fuzzy mould.

Too warm and it races and gets sharp; too cool (below about 20°C) and it can take weeks. An oven with only the light on is a good warm spot.
