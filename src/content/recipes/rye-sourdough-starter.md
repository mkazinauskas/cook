## Ingredients

- About 1 kg dark (wholemeal) rye flour
- Water, lukewarm (about 28°C). Use filtered or boiled-and-cooled tap water if yours is heavily chlorinated
- A clean 0.5-1 l glass jar with a loose lid, and a rubber band to mark the level

## Instructions

1. **Day 1:** In the jar, stir together 50 g rye flour and 50 g water into a thick paste. Scrape down the sides, put the lid on loosely and leave somewhere warm (25-28°C) for 24 hours.
2. **Day 2:** Stir in another 50 g rye flour and 50 g water. Leave another 24 hours.
3. **Day 3:** Discard all but 50 g of the starter. Add 50 g rye flour and 50 g water, stir well, scrape down the sides and mark the level with the rubber band.
4. **Day 4 onwards:** Repeat the step 3 feed once a day, at roughly the same time. An early burst of bubbles followed by a quiet day or two is normal. Keep feeding.
5. **Usually around day 5:** once the starter rises clearly within 12 hours of a feed, switch to two feeds a day, 12 hours apart, with the same amounts.
6. **Ready:** The starter is ready to bake with when it rises by at least half to double within 4-8 hours of a feed for 2-3 days in a row, the surface is covered in holes and cracks, and it smells sour and fruity. This usually takes 5-10 days.
7. To bake, feed it 6-12 hours before you need it and use it when it is risen and full of holes.

## Keeping the starter

- **Baking often:** keep it on the counter and feed twice a day (50 g starter + 50 g rye flour + 50 g water).
- **Baking weekly:** after a feed, let it rise for 1-2 hours, then keep it in the fridge. Feed once a week. The day before baking, take it out and give it 1-2 feeds at room temperature until it rises again.
- Grey liquid (hooch) on top just means it is hungry. Pour it off or stir it in and feed.

## Notes

A rye starter will not get domed and glossy like a wheat one, and it won't pass the float test: it is a heavy paste that rises and cracks. Watch the rubber band, not the texture.

Rye ferments faster and more reliably than wheat because the whole grain carries more wild yeast and enzymes. That is why it is the easiest starter for beginners.

It works in wheat bread too. To turn some into a wheat starter, feed it with strong bread flour instead of rye for 2-3 days.

Throw the starter out and start again if you see pink or orange streaks or fuzzy mould.
