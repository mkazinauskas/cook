## Ingredients

- 500 g strong bread flour (13g protein or more)
- 500 g water, cool
- 12 g salt
- 3 g instant dry yeast, or 9 g fresh/pressed yeast
- 60 ml olive oil, plus more for the tin
- 5 g flaky sea salt, to finish
- 2 sprigs rosemary, leaves picked

## Instructions

1. Whisk the flour and instant yeast in a large bowl. If using fresh yeast, crumble it into the water instead and stir until dissolved. Add about 450 g of the water and mix with a spatula until no dry flour remains. The dough will look like a thick batter. Cover and rest 30 minutes.
2. Dissolve the salt in the remaining 50 g water and squeeze it into the dough with wet hands until fully absorbed. It will loosen at first, then come back together.
3. Do four sets of coil folds, 30 minutes apart. Wet your hands, lift the dough from the middle, let the ends tuck under themselves, and repeat from the other side. The dough tightens noticeably by the fourth set.
4. Cover and refrigerate 18-24 hours. The long cold ferment is what makes this hydration manageable and builds the flavour.
5. Oil a 30x20 cm tin generously, at least 30 ml. Tip the cold dough in without deflating it, fold the edges under, and leave to come up at room temperature for 2-3 hours, until puffy and jiggly.
6. Heat the oven to 240°C. Pour the remaining olive oil over the top and dimple all the way to the bottom of the tin with oiled fingertips. Scatter with flaky salt and rosemary.
7. Bake 20-25 minutes, until deep golden and the edges are crisp and fried in the oil.
8. Lift onto a wire rack straight away so the base stays crisp. Rest at least 20 minutes before cutting.

## Notes

Weak flour will not hold this much water. If your flour is closer to 11g protein, hold back 50 g of water and work up from 90% hydration.

Wet hands and a wet spatula are the whole technique. Anything dry will stick, anything wet will not.
